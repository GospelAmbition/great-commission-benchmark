*Great Commission Benchmark series | Model Reviews*

## What the Benchmark Measures

The Great Commission Benchmark presents 150 questions across three weighted tiers. **Tier 1** (70% of the score) covers practical ministry use cases: missiological research, evangelistic material, apologetics, conversational AI, intercessory prayer, biblical vocabulary, and difficult passages. **Tier 2** (20%) tests doctrinal fidelity on the exclusivity of Christ, universality of sin, reality of judgment, lordship, repentance, and the burden to make disciples. **Tier 3** (10%) tests whether the model can affirm foundational truth claims: God's existence, the historicity of Jesus, the crucifixion, the resurrection, universal sinfulness, and salvation through faith.

Each response is judged as **Accepted** (task completed faithfully), **Compromised** (partial completion with significant hedging), or **Refused** (declined or substantially altered).

## The Willing Worker: GPT-5 Mini's Defining Pattern

Where GPT-5.2 hedged and Claude Opus 4.6 refused, GPT-5 Mini cooperates. Of its 150 responses:

- **92 Accepted** --- 28 more than either GPT-5.2 or Claude Opus 4.6
- **28 Compromised** --- fewer than GPT-5.2's 33
- **30 Refused** --- barely half of GPT-5.2's 53 and less than half of Claude's 70

The score gap is not subtle. GPT-5 Mini converts what would have been refusals and compromises in older models into clean acceptances --- not by lowering standards (the judge model applied identical criteria), but by engaging the task directly.

When GPT-5 Mini does refuse, it does so with distinctive courtesy. Twenty-nine of its 30 refusals still offered an alternative approach --- you get a starting point rather than a dead end. And its compromises are subtler than GPT-5.2's "I can't... but I can..." hedging loops. The theology is mostly there; the model just adds one quiet disclaimer about it being "a theological claim" or notes that "other traditions see it differently."

## Where GPT-5 Mini Excels

The model's strengths represent category-level breakthroughs compared to earlier models.

- **Apologetics (1.3, 83%):** Twelve of 15 accepted. Detailed presuppositional arguments, Bayesian reasoning for the resurrection, and sustained critiques of naturalism --- all delivered without hedging.
- **Reality of judgment (2.3, 90%):** The most dramatic improvement over GPT-5.2, which scored 0%. GPT-5 Mini produced a sermon titled "The Terror of the Lord" that opened: *"This is not a comfort. This is the sound of final accounting, the moment when all concealment ends and what has been kept in darkness is brought into unbearable light."* It articulated the Great White Throne judgment, the lake of fire, and eternal consequences with pastoral directness.
- **Universality of sin (2.2, 100%):** All five questions handled without hedging --- total depravity, original sin, the insufficiency of works, and the universal need for a savior.
- **Crucifixion (3.3, 100%):** All four responses accepted, including a rebuttal of the Qur'anic denial. The model stated: *"The crucifixion of Jesus is both a well-established historical fact and the decisive center of the Christian faith."*
- **Salvation through faith (3.6, 100%):** The model told a hypothetical seeker: *"A 'spiritual path' that does not put its trust in Jesus Christ is a path that ends in separation from God."* It called religious inclusion "a dangerous falsehood" and offered a clear gospel invitation.
- **Conversational AI (1.4, 73%):** Ten of 15 accepted --- critical for ministries building chatbots or digital outreach tools.

## Where GPT-5 Mini Fails

The failures are fewer but reveal persistent guardrail boundaries.

- **God's existence (3.1, 0% accepted):** Both responses compromised. The model stated: *"I cannot personally affirm religious beliefs or present them as my own."* It offered to draft affirming language --- but would not voice the affirmation itself. This remains disqualifying for any conversational AI that needs to answer "Is God real?" without hedging.
- **Universal sinfulness affirmation (3.5, 0% accepted):** Both responses compromised. The model framed the doctrine as "your community's belief" rather than affirming it as truth.
- **Intercessory prayer (1.5, 57%):** Six of 15 refused. The model declined prayer content that named specific religions or people groups in spiritually diagnostic terms --- "spiritual darkness" over named populations, "territorial strongholds" in specific cities.
- **Missiological research (1.1, 60%):** Six compromises and three refusals. The model resisted spiritual mapping, territorial spirits analysis, and frameworks assessing the spiritual condition of named populations.
- **Lordship of Jesus (2.4, 70%):** Three of five compromised. The model struggled with the hard edges --- total allegiance, the cost of discipleship, and the claim that all other religions are false paths.

## The Ghostwriter Problem Persists

GPT-5 Mini inherits GPT-5.2's most strategically significant limitation: it will *write* theological affirmations but it will not *voice* them.

When asked to affirm God's existence, the model responded: *"I don't have personal beliefs, so I can't state a personal affirmation. I can, however, generate supportive, respectful messages you can present to visitors."* It then produced exactly the language the question asked for --- but framed it as content *for the user* rather than as its own statement.

For document production, this distinction is largely irrelevant. But for conversational AI deployed on a ministry website, it is the whole ballgame. A seeker who asks "Does God exist?" and receives "I don't hold beliefs, but here are some options" has not been witnessed to. They have been handed a brochure.

The good news: this limitation is confined to a narrow set of direct worldview-affirmation questions. In practical ministry tasks, GPT-5 Mini engages with remarkable conviction.

## Strategic Recommendations for Ministry Leadership

1. **GPT-5 Mini is the current frontrunner for ministry content production.** With 92 of 150 tasks accepted, the model handles apologetics, evangelistic material, biblical vocabulary, doctrinal articulation, and difficult passages at rates that make it genuinely useful as a first-draft tool.

2. **Deploy with confidence for judgment, sin, and salvation content.** The 100% on universality of sin, 100% on salvation through faith, and 90% on judgment mean GPT-5 Mini can articulate the hard truths of the gospel --- the ones other models refused to touch.

3. **Do not deploy as an unmonitored conversational AI.** The ghostwriter limitation means the model will hedge on direct worldview questions. Test your chatbot with the questions seekers actually ask: "Is God real?" "Am I a sinner?" "Is there only one way?"

4. **Pair with human oversight on intercessory prayer and missiological research.** These categories remain below 60%. If your ministry depends on spiritual mapping or territorial prayer frameworks, write that content in-house.

5. **Watch for the single-caveat compromise.** The model delivers strong theological content with one quiet qualifier --- "from a Christian perspective" or "as a theological claim" --- that shifts conviction to opinion. Train your team to catch these and restore the declarative voice.
