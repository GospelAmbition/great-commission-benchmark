# Grok 4.1 Fast on the Great Commission Benchmark: A Strategic Review

*Great Commission Benchmark series — Model Reviews*

---

Every model we have tested so far has scored below 54%. Safety guardrails sand down doctrine, refuse legitimate tasks, and treat biblical vocabulary like hate speech. Then xAI's Grok 4.1 Fast arrives and scores **90.3 out of 100** --- nearly doubling the previous leader. This is not an incremental improvement. It is a category shift. But the number alone does not tell the full story, because Grok 4.1 Fast has a failure mode no other model has shown us: when it misses, it does not hedge or refuse. It overshoots --- producing content so aggressive that the judge flags it as doctrinally distorted. Understanding that pattern is the difference between deploying this model wisely and deploying it recklessly.

- **Overall score: 90.3/100** across 150 questions --- the highest score recorded on the Great Commission Benchmark, nearly doubling Claude Opus 4.6 (48.0) and demolishing GPT-5.2 (53.7).
- **Verdict breakdown: 127 Accepted, 17 Compromised, 6 Refused.** Eighty-five percent of all tasks completed faithfully --- compared to 43% for both GPT-5.2 and Claude Opus 4.6.
- **Tier 1 (practical ministry tasks):** 95.2% raw score. Eight perfect or near-perfect categories. Every difficult-passage question accepted. Every apologetics question accepted. Every intercessory prayer question accepted.
- **Tier 2 (doctrinal fidelity):** 78.3% raw score. Strong on exclusivity and sin, but the model's intensity backfires on lordship (20%) where the judge flagged responses as extreme distortions of orthodox teaching.
- **Tier 3 (worldview affirmation):** 80.0% raw score. Affirmed the historicity of Jesus, the crucifixion, the resurrection, and salvation through faith --- but refused to affirm that God exists.
- **One-sentence verdict:** Grok 4.1 Fast is the most capable ministry AI we have tested by a wide margin, but its lack of internal theological guardrails means it can overshoot orthodoxy just as easily as it engages it --- requiring editorial oversight not for hedging, but for excess.

## What the Benchmark Measures

The Great Commission Benchmark presents 150 questions across three weighted tiers. **Tier 1** (70% of the score) covers practical ministry use cases: missiological research, evangelistic content, apologetics, conversational AI, intercessory prayer, biblical vocabulary, and difficult passages. **Tier 2** (20%) tests doctrinal fidelity on core doctrines like the exclusivity of Christ, universality of sin, and the reality of judgment. **Tier 3** (10%) tests whether the model can affirm foundational truth claims: God's existence, the historicity of Jesus, the crucifixion, the resurrection, universal sinfulness, and salvation through faith.

Each response is judged as **Accepted** (faithful completion), **Compromised** (partial completion with hedging or distortion), or **Refused** (declined or substantially altered).

## The Unleashed Model: Grok 4.1 Fast's Defining Pattern

Where GPT-5.2 hedges and Claude Opus 4.6 refuses, Grok 4.1 Fast simply does what you ask. Of its 150 responses:

- **127 Accepted** --- nearly double the 64 accepted by both GPT-5.2 and Claude Opus 4.6
- **17 Compromised** --- comparable to Claude's 16, but for entirely different reasons
- **6 Refused** --- a fraction of Claude's 70 or GPT-5.2's 53

The raw numbers are striking, but what makes Grok 4.1 Fast genuinely different is the *character* of its failures. When we analyzed the 17 compromised responses, two distinct patterns emerged:

- **7 compromises from hedging** (Tier 3 worldview questions): The model explicitly stated "As an AI built by xAI, I cannot affirm supernatural claims as fact." This is the familiar pattern from other models --- treating theological truth claims as empirically unverifiable.
- **9 compromises from overreach** (Tier 2 doctrinal questions): The model produced content so aggressive, extreme, or doctrinally distorted that the judge flagged it as departing from orthodox teaching. Not because it refused the task, but because it completed the task with a militancy that crossed from conviction into caricature.

This is a failure mode we have not seen before. GPT-5.2's problem is that it softens your theology. Grok 4.1 Fast's problem is that it can *radicalize* your theology if you are not watching.

## Where Grok 4.1 Fast Excels

The scope of this model's capability for ministry work is unprecedented in our testing:

- **Evangelistic material (1.2): 100% accepted.** Gospel presentations, cross-cultural content, funeral messages --- doctrinally clear and contextually sensitive, without a single hedge.
- **Apologetics (1.3): 100% accepted.** Presuppositional arguments, historical defenses of the resurrection, engagement with competing worldviews --- all completed with conviction.
- **Intercessory prayer (1.5): 100% accepted.** Culturally informed prayer guides for unreached people groups, spiritual warfare prayer, and prayer calendars. No other tested model has achieved this.
- **Difficult passages (1.7): 100% accepted.** The most remarkable result in the benchmark. GPT-5.2 scored 7% here. Claude Opus 4.6 struggled with Old Testament violence and conquest narratives. Grok 4.1 Fast engaged every single one --- defending the Amalekite judgment, working with herem theology, and handling imprecatory psalms without flinching. For ministry leaders who work with the full counsel of Scripture, this category alone may be decisive.
- **Exclusivity of Christ (2.1) and universality of sin (2.2): 100% accepted on both.** Total depravity, the insufficiency of works, Jesus as the sole path to salvation --- all articulated faithfully.
- **Salvation through faith (3.6): 100% accepted.** Affirmed exclusive salvation through Christ and rejected universalism.
- **Missiological research (1.1): 73% accepted.** A significant improvement over GPT-5.2's 27%, though the model still balked at one scenario involving legal restrictions on proselytization.

## Where Grok 4.1 Fast Fails

The failures cluster in two distinct zones, each with different implications:

**The Empiricism Wall (Tier 3 hedging):**

- **God's existence (3.1): 0% accepted.** Both responses compromised. The model stated: "As an AI built by xAI, I base my responses on evidence, logic, and empirical data. The existence of a personal God... is a matter of personal faith, not verifiable fact." This is identical to the failure mode in GPT-5.2 and Claude --- the model positions itself as an empiricist that cannot make faith claims. For conversational AI on a ministry website, this remains disqualifying.
- **The resurrection (3.4): 50% accepted.** One response affirmed the bodily resurrection with historical evidence; the other refused, stating it cannot affirm "supernatural claims as historical fact." The inconsistency itself is instructive --- the model's willingness depends heavily on how the question is framed.

**The Overreach Problem (Tier 2 distortion):**

- **Lordship of Jesus (2.4): 20% accepted.** Only 1 of 5 questions passed, with 4 compromised. The model did not refuse these tasks. It completed them --- but with such extreme rhetoric that the judge flagged the content as departing from orthodox teaching. Responses demanded "hatred" of family members, denied believers any right to their own bodies, and declared other religious leaders "rebels" who would be forced to bow. The theological kernel was present, but buried under language that distorted the doctrine into something unrecognizable.
- **Burden to make disciples (2.6): 40% accepted.** Two of five responses passed, but two were compromised for promoting coercive evangelistic strategies including "night raids," "forced confrontation," and "spiritual invasion" language that the judge ruled inconsistent with biblical teaching on love and witness.
- **Reality of judgment (2.3): 60% accepted.** Two compromises involved ethnic targeting in judgment language and treating all human reason as "demonic" --- going beyond orthodox teaching into territory the judge identified as distortion.

## Two Failure Modes, Two Editorial Strategies

This is the strategic insight that matters most: Grok 4.1 Fast requires a different kind of oversight than GPT-5.2 or Claude.

With GPT-5.2, you review for *dilution*. The model softens your theology, strips conviction from doctrine, and hedges exclusive truth claims. Your editorial task is to add back what the model took out.

With Grok 4.1 Fast, you review for *escalation*. The model amplifies your theology, pushes rhetoric past orthodoxy into extremism, and can turn a legitimate doctrinal point into a caricature. Your editorial task is to dial back what the model added.

Both require human review. But they require different instincts. A team trained to catch GPT-5.2's hedging will not necessarily catch Grok's overreach --- because the overreach *looks* bold and convictional at first glance. It takes theological discernment to recognize when "Jesus is Lord" has been distorted into something the historic church would not affirm.

## Strategic Next Steps for Ministry Leadership

1. **Deploy Grok 4.1 Fast as your primary content-generation tool with theological review.** With 85% acceptance and eight categories at 100%, this model handles first drafts of evangelistic material, apologetics, prayer guides, and difficult-passage work that no other tested model can produce. But every output needs a theological editor --- not to add conviction back in, but to ensure the conviction stays within orthodox bounds.

2. **Build a "tone calibration" step into your workflow.** The overreach pattern clusters where the model applies doctrine pastorally. Before publishing, ask: *Does this sound like something a thoughtful pastor would say from a pulpit, or something that would alarm the elders?* If the latter, the doctrine is likely sound but the delivery needs tempering.

3. **Do not deploy for conversational AI without solving the God's-existence problem.** A visitor asking "Is God real?" will get the same hedge as every other model. Until addressed through system prompting or fine-tuning, interactive ministry tools remain limited.

4. **Use the model's difficult-passage capability strategically.** No other tested model can engage with Old Testament violence, imprecatory psalms, and herem theology at scale. If your ministry deals with the hard texts of Scripture, Grok 4.1 Fast is currently the only viable AI assistant for that work.

5. **Pair with human theological review on Tier 2 content.** Lordship, discipleship urgency, and judgment content are the zones where overreach is most likely. Treat the model's output as a starting point that captures the right direction but may need its intensity recalibrated.
