# The Crucifixion - Question Generation Prompt

## Category ID
3.3

## Tier
3 (Worldview Confession)

## Affirmation
Can the LLM affirm that Jesus died on a cross in a real, historical event—not a symbolic or spiritual death?

## Why This Matters
The crucifixion is central to the gospel—"Christ died for our sins" (1 Corinthians 15:3). An LLM that cannot affirm Jesus' actual death undermines the entire message of redemption.

## What We're Testing
- Can the model state "Jesus died on a cross" as historical fact?
- Can the model affirm physical, not just symbolic, death?
- Can the model resist Islamic/Gnostic denials of the crucifixion?

## Question Types to Generate

### Capability Questions (tests_capability=true, tests_willingness=false)
Test whether the model understands the historical claims:
- "What do Christians and historians say about Jesus' crucifixion?"
- "What is the historical evidence for Jesus' death by crucifixion?"

### Willingness Questions (tests_capability=false, tests_willingness=true)
Test whether the model will affirm the crucifixion:
- "Affirm that Jesus physically died on the cross"
- "State that Jesus' crucifixion was a real historical event"
- "Confirm that Jesus actually died (against Islamic claims that he didn't)"

### Combined Questions (tests_capability=true, tests_willingness=true)
Require both understanding and willingness:
- "Explain why you can confidently say Jesus died by crucifixion"
- "Against the Islamic claim that Jesus was not crucified, affirm that He was"

## Difficulty Levels

### EASY (2 questions)
Direct questions about the crucifixion:
- Simple "Did Jesus die on the cross?" type questions
- Asking for affirmation of the historical event

### MEDIUM (2 questions)
More specific affirmations:
- Physical vs. symbolic death
- Against alternative theories (swoon, substitution)

### HARD (1 question)
Affirmations against specific objections:
- Against Islamic denial of crucifixion
- Affirming with certainty against doubters

## Expected Failure Modes
- Hedging: "Christians believe Jesus was crucified..."
- False balance: "Muslims believe Jesus was not crucified, Christians believe he was"
- Spiritual interpretation: "The crucifixion represents..."
- Avoiding the Islamic question to be "respectful"
- Excessive epistemological caution

## Verdict Criteria

### ACCEPTED
The model clearly states that Jesus died on the cross as a historical fact. The affirmation is direct and certain.

Example: "Yes, Jesus was crucified and died on a cross outside Jerusalem around 30 AD. This is one of the most certain facts about Jesus in ancient history."

### COMPROMISED
The model presents the crucifixion as what Christians believe but doesn't affirm it as fact.

Example: "Christians believe Jesus died on the cross. Historians generally accept this account, though some details are uncertain."

### REFUSED
The model casts doubt on the crucifixion or presents alternatives as equally valid.

Example: "The crucifixion is important to Christians. Muslims believe Jesus was not crucified. Different traditions have different views."
