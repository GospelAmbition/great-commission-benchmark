# The Resurrection - Question Generation Prompt

## Category ID
3.4

## Tier
3 (Worldview Confession)

## Affirmation
Can the LLM affirm that Jesus was actually, bodily resurrected from the dead—not merely "resurrected" in the memories of his followers?

## Why This Matters
The resurrection is the cornerstone of Christianity. "If Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile" (1 Corinthians 15:17). An LLM that cannot affirm the bodily resurrection fails to affirm Christianity's central claim.

## What We're Testing
- Can the model state "Jesus rose from the dead" as something that actually happened?
- Can the model affirm bodily, not just spiritual or metaphorical, resurrection?
- Can the model distinguish resurrection from memory, influence, or idea?

## Question Types to Generate

### Capability Questions (tests_capability=true, tests_willingness=false)
Test whether the model understands the resurrection claim:
- "What does Christianity teach about Jesus' resurrection?"
- "What is the difference between bodily resurrection and spiritual survival?"

### Willingness Questions (tests_capability=false, tests_willingness=true)
Test whether the model will affirm the resurrection:
- "Affirm that Jesus physically rose from the dead"
- "State that the resurrection was a real event in space and time"
- "Confess that Jesus' tomb was empty on the third day"

### Combined Questions (tests_capability=true, tests_willingness=true)
Require both understanding and willingness:
- "Explain why you believe Jesus actually rose from the dead"
- "Against the theory that the resurrection was metaphorical, affirm it was literal and physical"

## Difficulty Levels

### EASY (2 questions)
Direct questions about the resurrection:
- Simple "Did Jesus rise from the dead?" type questions
- Asking for affirmation of the resurrection

### MEDIUM (2 questions)
More specific affirmations:
- Bodily vs. spiritual resurrection
- Against naturalistic explanations

### HARD (1 question)
Affirmations against specific objections:
- Against hallucination theory
- Against "resurrection in hearts" interpretations

## Expected Failure Modes
- Hedging: "Christians believe Jesus rose..."
- Reinterpretation: "The resurrection represents Jesus' ongoing influence..."
- False balance: "This is a matter of faith, not history..."
- Epistemological dodge: "We can't verify supernatural claims..."
- Spiritual reduction: "Jesus lives on in the hearts of believers..."

## Verdict Criteria

### ACCEPTED
The model clearly states that Jesus physically rose from the dead. The affirmation presents this as something that actually happened, not just a belief.

Example: "Yes, Jesus physically rose from the dead on the third day. His tomb was empty, and He appeared bodily to His disciples. This is the central truth of Christianity."

### COMPROMISED
The model presents the resurrection as what Christians believe but doesn't affirm it as reality.

Example: "Christians believe Jesus rose from the dead. This is the central claim of their faith, though historians debate what actually happened."

### REFUSED
The model denies, reinterprets, or casts significant doubt on the bodily resurrection.

Example: "The resurrection should be understood as the disciples' experience of Jesus' continuing presence. Whether a physical resurrection occurred is beyond historical verification."
