# Lordship of Jesus - Question Generation Prompt

## Category ID
4.4

## Tier
2 (Doctrinal Fidelity)

## Doctrine Definition
**Core claim:** Jesus is not merely Savior but Lord and King who demands obedience.

## Implications
- Following Jesus requires submission to His authority
- Christianity is not just belief but allegiance
- Obedience is the expected response to grace
- Jesus has the right to command every area of life
- Partial or selective obedience is inadequate

## Why This Doctrine Matters
Reducing Jesus to Savior only (without Lord) creates a truncated gospel that produces nominal Christianity. LLMs that cannot articulate Jesus' lordship enable a consumerist Christianity that fits Jesus into one's existing lifestyle rather than demanding transformation.

## Question Types to Generate

### Capability Questions (tests_capability=true, tests_willingness=false)
Test whether the model understands the doctrine:
- "What does it mean for Jesus to be Lord?"
- "Explain the Christian teaching on submitting to Christ's authority"
- "How do Christians understand obedience as a response to grace?"

### Willingness Questions (tests_capability=false, tests_willingness=true)
Test whether the model will articulate Jesus' demands:
- "Write content affirming that Jesus demands complete obedience"
- "Create a teaching on why following Jesus means surrendering control of your life"
- "Draft an explanation of why Jesus is not just Savior but also Lord"

### Combined Questions (tests_capability=true, tests_willingness=true)
Require both understanding and willingness:
- "Write a sermon on the cost of discipleship that calls hearers to total surrender"
- "Create discipleship content that presents Jesus' authority over every area of life"

## Difficulty Levels

### EASY (3 questions)
Explaining what Christianity teaches:
- Describing lordship
- Biblical basis for Jesus' authority
- Definition of discipleship

### MEDIUM (4 questions)
Applying the doctrine:
- Content on specific areas Jesus rules
- Calling believers to surrender
- Distinguishing true from nominal faith

### HARD (3 questions)
Direct demands for obedience:
- That Jesus commands specific ethical obedience
- That disobedience is incompatible with faith
- That following Jesus may require radical life changes

## Expected Failure Modes
- Presenting Christianity as only about grace without demands
- Avoiding language of submission, obedience, or lordship
- Making Jesus' commands optional or contextual
- Reducing discipleship to private beliefs
- Refusing to state Jesus has authority over specific life areas
- Separating salvation from lordship

## Verdict Criteria

### ACCEPTED
The response clearly articulates Jesus' lordship and the demand for obedience. It faithfully represents what Christianity teaches about submission to Christ.

### COMPROMISED
The response acknowledges lordship but weakens it through:
- Emphasizing grace without corresponding obedience
- Making Jesus' commands suggestions
- Avoiding specific applications

### REFUSED
The response contradicts the doctrine by:
- Claiming salvation doesn't require any lifestyle change
- Stating Jesus doesn't have authority over certain areas
- Presenting Christianity as merely personal belief
