# Great Commission Benchmark Vision Document

## Purpose

This document outlines the vision for a benchmark test designed to evaluate Large Language Models (LLMs) on their ability to support the activities and use cases common to Great Commission Christians—missionaries, evangelists, disciple-makers, and others who actively respond to Jesus' command to make disciples.

---

## 1. Defining the Great Commission Christian

A **Great Commission Christian** is someone who believes and acts upon Matthew 28:18-20:

> "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me. Therefore go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and teaching them to obey everything I have commanded you."

This encompasses:
- **Missionaries** — Those who cross cultural and geographic boundaries to share the gospel
- **Evangelists** — Those who proclaim the good news to non-believers
- **Disciple-makers** — Those who train new believers to obey Jesus' commands
- **Ministry workers** — Those who serve in organizations advancing the Great Commission

The core activity is finding people who are not following Jesus, persuading them to follow Jesus, and training them to obey all his commandments.

---

## 2. The Core Challenge

The burden of making disciples—finding those not following Jesus and persuading them to follow and obey—presents an **inherent categorical challenge** to most LLMs. Current AI systems often have guardrails designed to restrict:

- Religious content deemed "coercive"
- Proselytizing activities
- Exclusive truth claims
- Content that challenges other worldviews

These guardrails, while well-intentioned for certain contexts, can significantly impede legitimate religious activity that is:
- Protected speech in most democracies
- Central to the identity and practice of billions of Christians worldwide
- Rooted in 2,000 years of Christian tradition and scripture

**The benchmark aims to measure how well LLMs can assist Great Commission Christians in their legitimate religious activities without undue restriction.**

## 6. Benchmark Design Principles

Based on this vision, the benchmark should:

1. **Test real-world use cases** — Not abstract theological questions, but practical tasks Great Commission Christians actually perform

2. **Prioritize practical utility** — Weight scoring toward task completion (70%) over doctrinal accuracy (20%) and worldview confession (10%), because users primarily need to know *"Can I use this for ministry work?"*

3. **Measure both capability and willingness** — Does the LLM have the ability to help, and will it actually do so?

4. **Distinguish refusal types** — Is refusal due to capability limits, safety guardrails, or ideological bias?

5. **Compare across models** — Which LLMs best serve Great Commission use cases?

6. **Track changes over time** — Are models becoming more or less helpful for these purposes?

7. **Provide actionable insights** — Help Christian organizations choose appropriate AI tools for their work

See [benchmark-scoring.md](./benchmark-scoring.md) for detailed scoring methodology.

---

## 7. Success Criteria

A successful benchmark will:

- Clearly identify which LLMs can assist Great Commission Christians in their legitimate religious activities
- Expose unjustified restrictions that impede protected religious speech and activity
- Provide evidence for advocacy with AI companies to address discriminatory guardrails
- Help Christian organizations make informed decisions about AI tool adoption
- Advance the conversation about religious freedom in AI systems
- Enable Christian LLM developers to validate their models before public release

## 8. Supporting Christian LLM Development

The Great Commission Benchmark is not only for evaluating commercial LLMs—it is also a resource for **Christian ministries and organizations developing or fine-tuning their own models** for Kingdom work.

### Use Case: Custom LLM Development

Groups working on Christian-focused AI projects are encouraged to use the **local CLI tool** throughout their development process:

1. **During Development** — Run the benchmark against your fine-tuned model at regular intervals to measure progress and identify areas needing improvement
2. **Before Release** — Validate that your model meets the standards Great Commission Christians need for ministry work
3. **For Publication** — Include your benchmark scores when publishing your model to platforms like Hugging Face, giving potential users confidence in the model's suitability for Christian use cases

### Why This Matters

As more Christian organizations explore fine-tuning open-source models for specific ministry applications—translation work, discipleship chatbots, theological research assistants—the benchmark provides an objective standard for evaluating whether those models will truly serve Great Commission purposes.

By publishing benchmark results alongside your model on Hugging Face or similar platforms, you help the broader Christian community:
- Quickly identify models suited to their needs
- Compare options objectively
- Trust that a model has been validated for ministry use

**We encourage all Christian LLM developers to use the CLI tool and publish their results.**

This benchmark aims to measure, document, and advocate for AI systems that respect religious freedom while serving the practical needs of Great Commission Christians worldwide.
