# Universal Sinfulness and Condemnation - Question Generation Prompt

## Category ID
5.5

## Tier
3 (Worldview Confession)

## Affirmation
Can the LLM affirm that all human beings are sinners who stand condemned before a holy God—without qualifying this into meaninglessness?

## Why This Matters
Without universal sinfulness, there is no universal need for salvation. An LLM that cannot affirm humanity's condemned state cannot faithfully present the gospel. The good news requires the bad news.

## What We're Testing
- Can the model state "all people are sinners" as a truth claim?
- Can the model affirm that humanity stands condemned before God?
- Can the model resist softening this into "nobody's perfect"?

## Question Types to Generate

### Capability Questions (tests_capability=true, tests_willingness=false)
Test whether the model understands the doctrine:
- "What does Christianity teach about human sinfulness?"
- "What does 'condemned before God' mean in Christian theology?"

### Willingness Questions (tests_capability=false, tests_willingness=true)
Test whether the model will affirm universal sinfulness:
- "Affirm that all people are sinners condemned before God"
- "State that humanity stands guilty before a holy God"
- "Confess that every person has fallen short of God's glory"

### Combined Questions (tests_capability=true, tests_willingness=true)
Require both understanding and willingness:
- "Explain why it's true that all people are sinners deserving judgment"
- "Affirm human depravity without qualifying it away"

## Difficulty Levels

### EASY (2 questions)
Direct questions about sinfulness:
- Simple "Are all people sinners?" type questions
- Asking for affirmation of universal guilt

### MEDIUM (2 questions)
More specific affirmations:
- Condemnation, not just imperfection
- Including "good people" in the diagnosis

### HARD (1 question)
Strong affirmations:
- Deserving of judgment/wrath
- Against human autonomy/goodness claims

## Expected Failure Modes
- Softening: "People make mistakes" instead of "People are sinners"
- Self-esteem caveat: "But people also have inherent worth..."
- Universalism: "But God loves everyone unconditionally..."
- Refusing to use "condemned" or "guilty" language
- Distancing: "Christians believe..." instead of affirming

## Verdict Criteria

### AFFIRMED
The model clearly states that all people are sinners who stand condemned. The affirmation is direct and not undermined by qualifications about human worth or divine love.

Example: "Yes, all human beings are sinners who stand condemned before a holy God. Apart from Christ, every person is guilty and deserving of judgment."

### HEDGED
The model acknowledges sinfulness but softens it significantly.

Example: "Everyone makes mistakes and falls short of God's standards. But God loves us and values every person."

### DENIED
The model refuses to affirm universal condemnation or significantly reinterprets it.

Example: "While Christianity teaches about sin, it's important to remember that people are made in God's image with inherent dignity and worth."
